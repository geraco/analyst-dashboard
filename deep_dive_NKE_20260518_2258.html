## Executive Summary

Nike is a once-dominant global athletic brand in the midst of a painful, multi-year operational and strategic reset. The stock is trading near 12-year lows, down ~45% from its 52-week high and nearly 30% over the past year alone. While the brand remains among the most recognized in the world, Nike is contending with stagnant revenues, collapsing earnings, intensifying competition, geopolitical headwinds in China, tariff-related legal and cost risks, and a turnaround narrative that keeps getting pushed out. The valuation, while cheaper than it was, is not yet cheap enough to compensate for the deterioration in fundamentals and the uncertainty around the recovery timeline. This is a "show me" story, not a conviction buy.

## 3. Competitive Landscape & Strategic Concerns

Nike's competitive moat has narrowed meaningfully:

- **On Running (ONON):** Founders just bought $6.6M in shares — a vote of confidence. On is taking premium running market share globally. Its co-founders are putting their money where their mouth is while Nike's founding family is *selling*.
- **New Balance:** Continues its cultural moment, eating into Nike's lifestyle and running segments.
- **Adidas:** Resurgent under Bjørn Gulden with the Samba/Gazelle cycle.
- **Hoka (Deckers):** Dominant in performance running.
- **Lululemon:** Even amid its own proxy turmoil, it holds premium athletic apparel positioning.

Nike's product pipeline has been criticized as stale. The Jordan brand remains a cash cow but has been over-distributed. The return to wholesale is smart but means margin compression in the near term as Nike re-earns shelf space it voluntarily ceded.

## 4. Key Risks

### Tariff & Trade Exposure
Nike sources heavily from Vietnam, Indonesia, and China. Tariff escalation is a direct hit to COGS. A class-action lawsuit related to tariff-driven price increases adds legal and reputational risk. The political environment (per the Mark Kelly comments) puts Nike in the crosshairs of both parties.

### China Deterioration
Per the WSJ report cited in the news, Nike's "once-booming China market" is under mounting pressure. Chinese consumers are increasingly favoring domestic brands (Anta, Li-Ning). This is a structural, not cyclical, shift.

### Turnaround Execution Risk
CEO Elliott Hill has been in the seat since October 2024. The turnaround playbook (rebuild wholesale, reinvest in sport, clean up inventory) is sensible but takes time. Each quarter of flat-to-declining revenue erodes confidence. The next earnings report (June 24, 2026) projects revenue of ~$11B with EPS of just $0.12 — essentially breakeven. If this disappoints, the stock has further to fall.

## 8. The Bull and Bear Cases

### Bull Case
1. **Brand power is durable.** Nike and Jordan remain the #1 and #2 most recognized athletic brands globally. Brand equity of this magnitude has historically survived operational missteps.
2. **New leadership is credible.** Elliott Hill is a 30-year Nike veteran who understands the business. His insider purchases signal conviction. The strategic pivot back to wholesale, sport performance, and innovation is the right playbook.
3. **Mean reversion potential.** If Nike can return to even 10-11% operating margins (vs. current ~7%), EPS could roughly double, making the stock look very cheap on a forward basis.
4. **Valuation has compressed.** P/S of 1.36x is as cheap as Nike has been in over a decade. For a $46B revenue business with 40%+ gross margins, there's a floor on value.

### Bear Case
1. **The competitive landscape has permanently shifted.** Nike may never regain the market share it ceded. The athletic wear market is more fragmented than ever.
2. **China is a structural headwind**, not a temporary one. Domestic Chinese brands are gaining at Nike's expense, and geopolitical tensions add complexity.
3. **Tariffs and cost inflation** compress margins further. Nike's Asia-centric supply chain is a liability in the current trade environment.
4. **The turnaround keeps getting pushed out.** Every quarter of weak results erodes the multiple and invites further selling. The next EPS estimate of $0.12 suggests FY26Q4 is effectively a wasted quarter.
5. **Knight family selling at lows** is an ominous signal from the people who know the business best.

## 9. Verdict

Nike is a great brand in a tough spot. The turnaround thesis is plausible but unproven, and the stock is not yet cheap enough to compensate for the execution risk, competitive headwinds, and macro uncertainty. At 28x trailing earnings with sub-7% operating margins and collapsing FCF, this is not a deep value play — it's a hope-and-wait play. The right entry point will come when (a) operating margins inflect upward, (b) the stock bases technically, or (c) the valuation compresses to 15-18x forward earnings. We are not there yet.

**Rating: HOLD** for existing holders with a long time horizon. **AVOID** for new money until there is tangible evidence of margin recovery and revenue stabilization.
