# Exelon Corporation (EXC) — Equity Research Report

## Executive Summary

Exelon is the largest regulated electric utility holding company in the United States by customer count, serving approximately 10 million customers across six utilities (ComEd, PECO, BGE, Pepco, Delmarva Power, and Atlantic City Electric). Following the 2022 spin-off of its competitive generation business (now Constellation Energy), Exelon is a pure-play regulated utility — a simpler, more predictable business, but one whose upside is fundamentally capped by regulatory outcomes and capital deployment efficiency. The stock trades at roughly fair value versus its sector, with a few notable concerns beneath the surface.

---

## Valuation Assessment

Exelon's trailing P/E of 16.2x is essentially in line with the sector average of 16.2x and its own 5-year average. The forward P/E of 14.6x suggests modest earnings growth is priced in, but this is not a discounted entry point by any means. EV/EBITDA at 11.6x is reasonable but not cheap for a regulated utility.

**The Price/Free Cash Flow of 275.6x is a glaring red flag.** Free cash flow is deeply negative at -€2.8B, which is characteristic of a utility in heavy capital spending mode — but it means the dividend (which at a reported 383% yield appears to be a data error, likely reflecting a special distribution or data feed issue) is being funded by debt issuance, not organic cash flow. This is a structural concern.

Price/Book at 1.55x is moderate, suggesting the market is not pricing in excessive growth but is paying a premium to book value that needs to be justified by earned returns above cost of equity — ROE at 9.76% is adequate but not exceptional for a regulated utility.

---

## Financial Health — The Elephant in the Room

**Debt is the dominant issue for Exelon.** Total debt of €51.2B against total cash of just €713M and equity implying a Debt/Equity ratio of 174.8% is extremely elevated. For context, this is high even by utility standards. The current ratio of 0.94 and quick ratio of 0.60 indicate thin liquidity.

The company just raised its capex plan to $41.7B through 2029, which will require continued capital market access. In a rising or elevated interest rate environment, this poses meaningful refinancing and cost-of-capital risk. Operating cash flow of €6.8B is solid, but it is being more than consumed by capital expenditures, resulting in persistently negative free cash flow.

**The critical question for Exelon investors is whether regulators will allow the company to earn adequate returns on its massive and growing rate base.** If regulatory outcomes disappoint — which is a real risk given the company's own acknowledgment of "affordability issues" — the returns on this capital may compress.

---

## Growth & Strategy

Revenue grew 7.9% YoY in Q1 2026, driven by higher rates, favorable weather, and growing electricity demand (partly from data center load). The company raised its capex plan, with a notable shift toward transmission spending and away from distribution operations. This is strategically sound — transmission investments generally earn more predictable, FERC-regulated returns compared to state-level distribution rate cases.

However, earnings growth was essentially flat (-0.3% YoY), meaning higher revenues are being absorbed by higher costs, depreciation, and interest expense. The 3-year EPS CAGR of 7.55% is decent but needs to be sustained, and the path to continued EPS growth requires successful regulatory execution across multiple jurisdictions.

The data center demand narrative is real but should not be overstated. Exelon's service territories (especially ComEd in northern Illinois and Pepco in the D.C. metro area) are well-positioned for data center load growth, but the timeline and magnitude of incremental earnings contribution are uncertain.

---

## Recent Earnings & News

Q1 2026 results were solid: $0.91 EPS vs. $0.8875 estimate (a modest beat), revenue of $7.24B vs. $6.91B estimate. However, the stock fell ~1% post-earnings, suggesting the beat was expected and/or the market is focused on the capex/leverage trajectory.

Management's commentary about lowering utility spending to "ease affordability issues" is notable. This is a tacit admission that customer bill pressure is becoming a political and regulatory constraint. Utilities that push rates too aggressively risk adverse regulatory outcomes — rate case disallowances, lower allowed ROEs, or delayed cost recovery.

## Dividend Assessment

The reported 383% dividend yield is almost certainly a data error — likely reflecting the Constellation spin-off distribution or a feed calculation issue. Exelon's actual annual dividend is approximately $1.52/share (recently raised), giving a yield around 3.4%. This is competitive for the utility sector but not exceptional. Dividend growth should track EPS growth of 5-7%, which is the company's stated target. The payout ratio appears manageable on an earnings basis, but the deeply negative free cash flow means the dividend is effectively debt-funded.

---

## Risk Assessment

1. **Regulatory risk**: Exelon operates across six state jurisdictions plus FERC. Any adverse rate case outcome could materially impact earnings. The affordability narrative increases this risk.
2. **Balance sheet risk**: With $51B+ in debt and an aggressive capex plan, Exelon is highly leveraged and dependent on capital markets access. A credit downgrade would meaningfully increase financing costs.
3. **Interest rate sensitivity**: As a capital-intensive, dividend-paying utility with a low beta (0.42), EXC is a bond proxy. Rising long-term rates compress the relative attractiveness of the dividend and increase borrowing costs.
4. **Execution risk**: A $41.7B capex program requires disciplined project management, timely regulatory approvals, and workforce availability. Cost overruns or delays are inevitable at this scale.

---

## Verdict

Exelon is a fundamentally sound, well-run regulated utility trading at fair value. The Q1 beat was modest, the data center demand tailwind is real but priced in, and the balance sheet is stretched. The stock is 12% below its 52-week high and 8% above its low — it's in no-man's land technically. For income-oriented investors already holding, this is a reasonable HOLD. For new money, there's no compelling reason to rush in at current levels; better entry points are plausible if interest rates rise further or if regulatory headwinds materialize. The risk/reward is neutral.
