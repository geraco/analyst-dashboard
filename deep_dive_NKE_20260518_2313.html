# Nike, Inc. (NKE) — Deep-Dive Equity Research Report

**Date:** May 18, 2026
**Current Price:** $42.57 | **Market Cap:** ~$63B | **Rating: HOLD (with caution)**

## Executive Summary

Nike is a once-dominant global athletic brand that is in the midst of a multi-year operational and strategic deterioration. The stock is trading near its 52-week low, down ~45% from its high, and ~30% over the past year. The fundamentals tell a story of stagnating revenue, collapsing earnings, margin compression, and growing competitive threats. While the brand retains enormous global recognition and the balance sheet is not in distress, Nike faces serious structural headwinds that make a near-term recovery uncertain. The stock is not yet cheap enough to compensate for the risk of further deterioration.

---

## I. Valuation Assessment

Nike trades at a **trailing P/E of 28x** and a **forward P/E of 23x**, both above the sector median of ~19x. This is notable given that Nike's earnings are *declining*, not growing — EPS has contracted at a **-16.8% 3-year CAGR**, and YoY earnings fell **-34.8%**. Paying a premium multiple for a company with deteriorating earnings is a classic value trap signal.

- **P/S of 1.36x and EV/Revenue of 1.40x** are historically cheap for Nike but reflect the market's justified skepticism about margin recovery.
- **Price/Free Cash Flow of ~60x** is extremely elevated, indicating that the current cash generation run-rate does not support the valuation.
- **EV/EBITDA of 16.9x** is not demanding for a premium consumer brand, but it assumes margins will stabilize or recover — an assumption not yet supported by data.

**Bottom line:** Nike is not expensive on revenue-based metrics, but it is expensive relative to its current (and potentially further declining) earnings power. The valuation only works if you believe in a meaningful earnings recovery, which requires faith in a turnaround that has not yet materialized.

## II. Fundamental Analysis

### Revenue & Growth
Revenue is essentially flat (0.1% YoY growth, -0.3% 3-year CAGR). For a company that was growing at high single digits historically, this is a dramatic deceleration. Nike has lost market share to newer competitors (On Running, Hoka/Deckers, New Balance, and Asics) and has struggled with its direct-to-consumer strategy execution.

### Margins
- **Gross margin of 40.9%** remains respectable but is below Nike's historical norm of 44-46%. Tariff pressures, discounting to clear inventory, and input cost inflation have weighed.
- **Operating margin of 6.9%** is alarmingly low for Nike — this was routinely 12-15% in healthier periods. SG&A has not been rationalized proportional to the revenue stall.
- **Net margin of 4.8%** is weak for a premium brand company.
- **EBITDA margin of 8.3%** tells the same story: this is a company operating well below its structural margin potential.

### Earnings Trajectory
The **-34.8% YoY earnings decline** is severe. The upcoming Q4 FY2026 EPS estimate of $0.12 is barely above breakeven, suggesting the bottom may not yet be in. If margin recovery is slower than expected, we could see further estimate cuts.

### Balance Sheet
The balance sheet is adequate but not a fortress:
- **$8.1B cash vs. $11.2B debt** = ~$3.1B net debt. Manageable.
- **Debt/Equity of 79%** is elevated but not dangerous for a company of Nike's scale.
- **Current ratio of 2.14** indicates no liquidity stress.
- **Free cash flow of $1.3B** is a shadow of its former self (~$5-6B in peak years). The company is still generating cash, but the trend is clearly negative.

### ROE & Capital Returns
ROE of 16% looks decent but is partially a function of elevated leverage and a shrinking equity base, not operational excellence.

### Dividend
The dividend yield shown as 392% is clearly a data error — Nike's actual annualized dividend is approximately $1.48/share, implying a yield of ~3.5% at current prices. This is historically high for Nike and signals the market is pricing in risk to the dividend's sustainability if earnings continue to erode.

## III. Competitive Landscape & Strategic Concerns

This is where the story gets uncomfortable for Nike bulls:

1. **Market Share Loss:** On Running, Hoka, New Balance, and Asics have all taken meaningful share in the running and lifestyle categories. Nike's innovation pipeline has been perceived as stale — relying too heavily on retro/heritage models (Dunks, Air Force 1s) that are now oversaturated.

2. **China Headwinds:** Per the WSJ report from May 12, Nike faces "mounting pressure" in China — once a key growth engine. Local competitors (Anta, Li-Ning) have gained, and geopolitical/consumer sentiment dynamics have shifted unfavorably.

3. **DTC Strategy Missteps:** Nike's aggressive push to direct-to-consumer alienated wholesale partners. The company has been forced to re-engage wholesale channels, but the damage to relationships and shelf space is not quickly reversed.

4. **Tariff/Trade Risk:** With Nike's heavy reliance on Asian manufacturing (Vietnam, China, Indonesia), tariff policy changes represent a material margin risk. A class-action lawsuit has already been filed related to tariff-driven pricing.

5. **New CEO (Elliott Hill):** Hill, a Nike veteran who returned in late 2024, has acknowledged the problems and is implementing a turnaround plan. But turnarounds take time — typically 4-6 quarters minimum before results show in financials.

## VIII. Investment Verdict

### The Bull Case
Nike is a generational brand trading at a historically low valuation on revenue metrics. A new CEO with deep institutional knowledge is executing a turnaround. Insider buying by the CEO and board directors signals internal confidence. If margins recover even partway to historical norms (say, operating margin returns to 10-12%), the stock could see significant upside from current levels. Nike still has the largest global distribution network in athletic footwear and apparel.

### The Bear Case
Earnings are in freefall with no clear trough in sight. Revenue is flat-to-declining. Market share losses to nimble competitors may be structural, not cyclical. China — once the growth savior — is now a headwind. Tariff risks are material and unresolved. The valuation is not cheap relative to current earnings power. The founding family is selling aggressively.

### My Assessment
Nike is not a broken company, but it is a **broken stock with broken near-term fundamentals**. Turnarounds in large consumer brands can work, but they are slow and uncertain. The stock is not yet pricing in a worst-case scenario — at 23x forward earnings on potentially still-too-high estimates, there is room for further downside if Q4 FY2026 disappoints or guidance is weak.

I would rate this a **HOLD** for existing shareholders who have conviction in the 2-3 year turnaround thesis, and an **AVOID** for new money until there is clearer evidence of (a) a revenue inflection, (b) margin stabilization, or (c) a more compelling valuation (sub-$35 would change the calculus materially).
